Voice describes whether the subject of a sentence performs or receives the action. In **Active Voice**, the subject acts. In **Passive Voice**, the subject is acted upon.

### 1. When to Use Passive Voice
- When the actor is unknown, unimportant, or obvious: *"The village road was repaired last week."*
- When focusing on the recipient or result: *"Scholarships were awarded by the school committee."*

### 2. Basic Rules of Conversion
1. The object of the active sentence becomes the subject of the passive sentence.
2. Use the matching form of **be** followed by the **Past Participle (V3)** of the main verb.
3. Add **by + agent** at the end (can be omitted if vague, like *someone* or *people*).

### 3. Quick Tense Conversion Reference
- **Simple Present:** am / is / are + V3 (*He writes letters $\rightarrow$ Letters are written by him.*)
- **Present Continuous:** am / is / are + being + V3 (*She is cooking $\rightarrow$ Food is being cooked.*)
- **Simple Past:** was / were + V3 (*They built a bridge $\rightarrow$ A bridge was built.*)
- **Present Perfect:** has / have + been + V3 (*We have planted trees $\rightarrow$ Trees have been planted.*)
- **Modals:** modal + be + V3 (*You must obey rules $\rightarrow$ Rules must be obeyed.*)

### Illustrative Examples
1. Active: Dhan Bahadur Sir teaches English to grade ten.
   Passive: English is taught to grade ten by Dhan Bahadur Sir.
2. Active: The students organized a successful science exhibition.
   Passive: A successful science exhibition was organized by the students.
3. Active: The municipality has constructed a modern library.
   Passive: A modern library has been constructed by the municipality.
4. Active: The gardener is watering the flowerbeds.
   Passive: The flowerbeds are being watered by the gardener.
5. Active: We must preserve our natural heritage.
   Passive: Our natural heritage must be preserved.
6. Active: Someone stole the bicycle yesterday.
   Passive: The bicycle was stolen yesterday.
