Tense indicates the time of an action or state of being. English verbs change form across three main timeframes: **Present**, **Past**, and **Future**.

### 1. Simple Present Tense (Habits and Facts)
- **Structure:** Subject + Verb (V1 / V5) + Object.
- **Usage:** Daily routines, habitual actions, and universal truths.
- *Examples:* "The earth revolves around the sun." / "He walks to school every morning."

### 2. Present Continuous Tense (In Progress)
- **Structure:** Subject + is / am / are + V4 (-ing) + Object.
- *Example:* "The teacher is explaining a new grammar lesson."

### 3. Simple Past Tense (Completed Past Actions)
- **Structure:** Subject + Verb (V2) + Object.
- **Usage:** Events completed at a specific time in the past.
- *Example:* "We visited Janakpur last winter."

### 4. Present Perfect Tense
- **Structure:** Subject + has / have + Verb (V3) + Object.
- **Usage:** Completed actions with present relevance.
- *Example:* "I have submitted my homework to the teacher."

### 5. Simple Future Tense
- **Structure:** Subject + will / shall + Verb (V1) + Object.
- *Example:* "Our school will conduct exams next month."

### Illustrative Examples
1. She **teaches** English literature with great enthusiasm.
2. The students **are practicing** debate in the hall right now.
3. Last Friday, our football team **won** the championship.
4. Dhan Bahadur Sir **has published** articles on rural education.
5. They **will plant** fifty saplings around the school tomorrow.
6. The water in this kettle **is boiling** rapidly.
