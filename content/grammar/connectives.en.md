Connectives (linking words or conjunctions) join words, phrases, and clauses together, establishing logical flow and cohesion between thoughts. Understanding connectives enables learners to construct coherent sentences.

### 1. Connectives of Contrast
Highlight unexpected differences or opposing ideas.
- **Although / Even though:** Followed by a complete clause (*"**Although** it rained, they finished the match."*).
- **Despite / In spite of:** Followed by a noun or -ing form (*"**Despite** the rain, they finished the match."*).
- **However:** Introduces a contrasting statement (*"He worked hard; **however**, he failed."*).

### 2. Connectives of Reason and Cause
- **Because / As / Since:** Followed by a clause (*"She passed **because** she studied hard."*).
- **Due to / Because of:** Followed by a noun phrase (*"Delayed **due to** heavy rain."*).

### 3. Connectives of Purpose and Result
- **So that:** Explains purpose (*"He ran fast **so that** he could catch the bus."*).
- **Therefore / Consequently:** Shows result (*"The road was blocked; **therefore**, we walked."*).

### Illustrative Examples
1. **Although** she was nervous, her speech impressed the examiners.
2. He walked briskly to the bus stop **so that** he would not be late.
3. The match was cancelled **owing to** bad weather conditions.
4. Ramesh read many books; **consequently**, his English improved.
5. **In spite of** financial difficulties, she completed her college degree.
6. We must protect our forests **because** they give us clean air.
