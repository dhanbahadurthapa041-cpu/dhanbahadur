Conditional sentences describe what happens, what might happen, or what we wish would happen under specific circumstances. They consist of an **if-clause** (condition) and a **main clause** (result).

### 1. Zero Conditional (Facts and Truths)
- **Structure:** If + Simple Present, ... Simple Present.
- *Example:* "If you heat water to 100°C, it boils." / "If ice gets warm, it melts."

### 2. First Conditional (Real Future Possibility)
- **Structure:** If + Simple Present, ... will / can / may + V1.
- *Example:* "If you study hard, you will pass the examination." / "If it rains, we will stay home."

### 3. Second Conditional (Hypothetical Present)
- **Structure:** If + Simple Past (were / V2), ... would / could + V1.
- *Example:* "If I won a lottery, I would build a school library." / "If I were a bird, I would fly high."

### 4. Third Conditional (Unreal Past and Regrets)
- **Structure:** If + Past Perfect (had + V3), ... would have + V3.
- *Example:* "If she had left earlier, she would have caught the morning bus."

### Illustrative Examples
1. If you boil clean water, it **evaporates** into steam.
2. If our team **wins** tomorrow, the school **will celebrate**.
3. If I **were** an astronaut, I **would travel** to the moon.
4. If he **had listened** to advice, he **would not have failed**.
5. If plants **receive** regular sunlight, they **grow** strong.
6. If she **had studied** medicine, she **would have become** a doctor.
