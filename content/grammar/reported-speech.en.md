Reported speech (indirect speech) is used to convey what another person said without quoting their exact words verbatim. Changing direct speech into indirect speech involves shifts in tense, pronouns, and time expressions.

### 1. General Transformation Rules
1. **Reporting Verb:** Change *said to* into *told* when followed by an object. Keep *said* if there is no object.
2. **Conjunction:** Use **that** for statements, **if / whether** for yes/no questions, and the **wh-word** itself for wh-questions.
3. **Pronoun Changes:** First person pronouns shift according to the subject of the reporting verb, while second person pronouns shift according to the object.

### 2. Tense Shifts (Past Reporting Verb)
- Simple Present (V1/V5) $\rightarrow$ Simple Past (V2)
- Present Continuous (is/am/are) $\rightarrow$ Past Continuous (was/were)
- Simple Past (V2) $\rightarrow$ Past Perfect (had + V3)
- Present Perfect $\rightarrow$ Past Perfect (had + V3)
- Will / Can $\rightarrow$ Would / Could

*Note:* Universal truths remain unchanged ("The teacher said that water boils at 100°C").

### 3. Time and Place Changes
- *today* $\rightarrow$ *that day*; *yesterday* $\rightarrow$ *the day before*; *tomorrow* $\rightarrow$ *the next day*; *now* $\rightarrow$ *then*.

### Illustrative Examples
1. Direct: He said, "I am learning English grammar."
   Indirect: He said that he was learning English grammar.
2. Direct: Gita said to me, "I bought a dictionary yesterday."
   Indirect: Gita told me that she had bought a dictionary the day before.
3. Direct: The teacher said, "The earth is round."
   Indirect: The teacher said that the earth is round.
4. Direct: Ram asked Sita, "Do you like classical music?"
   Indirect: Ram asked Sita if she liked classical music.
5. Direct: The doctor said to the patient, "Take this medicine twice daily."
   Indirect: The doctor advised the patient to take that medicine twice daily.
6. Direct: She asked, "Where do you live?"
   Indirect: She asked me where I lived.
