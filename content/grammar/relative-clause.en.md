A relative clause is a subordinate clause that modifies a noun or pronoun, providing essential or supplementary details about people, things, places, or possessions. Relative clauses are introduced by **relative pronouns** (*who*, *whom*, *whose*, *which*, *that*) or **relative adverbs** (*where*, *when*).

### 1. Relative Pronouns Usage
- **Who:** Refers to people as subjects (*"The teacher **who** guides us is kind."*).
- **Whom:** Refers to people as objects (*"The doctor **whom** we consulted was experienced."*).
- **Whose:** Shows possession (*"The boy **whose** bicycle was stolen was sad."*).
- **Which:** Refers to things and animals (*"The book **which** is on the desk is mine."*).
- **That:** Used for people, things, or animals in defining clauses.
- **Where / When:** Denotes places and times (*"The village **where** I grew up."* / *"The day **when** we met."*).

### 2. Defining vs. Non-Defining Clauses
- **Defining Clauses:** Essential to identify the noun. No commas used.
  - *"Students **who work hard** achieve good marks."*
- **Non-Defining Clauses:** Add extra information about a known subject. Commas are mandatory.
  - *"Mr. Thapa, **who is our head teacher**, encourages reading."*

### Illustrative Examples
1. The student **who** won first prize received an educational scholarship.
2. We visited the ancient temple **which** stands on the hilltop.
3. The girl **whose** mother is a nurse won the essay competition.
4. Pokhara is a scenic city **where** thousands of tourists visit.
5. The book **that** you recommended taught me useful skills.
6. The morning **when** our library opened was truly memorable.
