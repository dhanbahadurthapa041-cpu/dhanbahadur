Subject-verb agreement (concord) means that the grammatical subject and verb of a sentence must agree in number (singular or plural) and person.

### 1. The Cardinal Rule
- A singular subject takes a singular verb: *"The student **reads** attentively."*
- A plural subject takes a plural verb: *"The students **read** attentively."*

### 2. Crucial Concord Rules
1. **Joined by 'And':** Two subjects joined by **and** take a plural verb (*"Gold and silver **are** precious metals."*). If they express a single concept, use a singular verb (*"Bread and butter **is** wholesome breakfast."*).
2. **With 'As well as', 'Together with':** The verb agrees strictly with the **first subject** (*"The principal, together with teachers, **is** coming."*).
3. **Either...Or / Neither...Nor:** The verb agrees with the subject **nearest** to it (*"Neither the teacher nor the students **were** present."*).
4. **Indefinite Pronouns:** *Each*, *every*, *everyone*, *nobody* take a **singular verb** (*"Each of the boys **receives** a prize."*).
5. **Measurements and Amounts:** Specific quantities of money, distance, or time take a singular verb (*"Ten kilometers **is** a long walk."*).

### Illustrative Examples
1. The captain, along with his teammates, **was** felicitated on stage.
2. Every boy and girl in the school **has** a designated desk.
3. Ten kilometers **is** a long distance to travel on foot.
4. Neither the monitor nor the prefects **are** responsible for the noise.
5. The list of successful candidates **was** published on the board.
6. Fifty thousand rupees **was** allocated for library books.

### Practice Exercise
Choose the correct verb form from the options given:
1. Either my brother or my parents _______ (is / are) attending the meeting.
2. Bread and butter _______ (is / are) his favourite breakfast.
3. Each of the ten computers _______ (has / have) internet access.
4. The head teacher, accompanied by staff members, _______ (has / have) arrived.
5. Mathematics _______ (is / are) an interesting subject for inquisitive minds.
